01 — Meet Shawn

A neighbour, not a stranger.

I grew up here. One of six children in Scarborough–Rouge Park, raised in a neighbourhood where we looked out for one another and where hard work was rewarded.

In 2008, I founded Matrix Mortgage Global, building it into Canada's largest private lending brokerage and creating jobs for dozens of our neighbours.

As former President of the Scarborough Business Association, I earned the trust of thousands of residents and business owners across Scarborough–Rouge Park.

I established the Shawn Allen Award for students in the Faculty of Management and International Business at UTSC — because a community that invests in its young people is building its own future.

My wife and I have raised five children here. When thousands of neighbours trusted me to represent our community in 2025, I stepped forward, and continued to build something very special.

Now, I am asking that we finish what we started together — and send someone to City Hall who is committed to staying there, and relentlessly fighting for our community.

02 — My Priorities

Three commitments to Scarborough–Rouge Park.

A councillor's job is not to be everywhere on every issue — it is to deliver on the things that decide whether this community thrives. Here is where I am focused.

01

Accountable, transparent spending on what residents actually need.

Every dollar at City Hall belongs to the people of Toronto. Residents deserve to see where their property taxes go, why projects cost what they cost, and what the measurable return is for our community. I will push for line-item transparency on every capital project that touches Ward 25, and I will say no when the numbers don't justify the spend.

02

Real, measurable improvements to the connections our community depends on.

Our families need a transit system that actually works — buses that run on time, the Eglinton East LRT delivered to Malvern, and roads that don't strand commuters. I will measure progress in trip times and reliability, not announcements. Connectivity is the difference between an opportunity reached and an opportunity lost.

03

The Rouge National Urban Park is ours to safeguard — for our children's generation.

The Rouge is the largest urban park in North America, and it sits in our backyard. Protecting it means more than a name on a map — it means active stewardship of its watersheds, its trails, and the communities that border it. I will be a councillor who shows up for the Rouge, not one who treats it as scenery.

07 — Volunteer

Join the team.

Campaigns are won at the doorstep, on the phone, and in the hours between dinner and 9 p.m. If you can give us an evening a week, a weekend, or just election day — we'll put you to work.

Where we need help.

Every volunteer is briefed, paired with experienced organizers, and matched to a role that fits their schedule and comfort level. No experience required.

  • 01
    Door-to-door canvassing Weekends and weeknight evenings across all 35 polls in the ward. The single highest-impact activity.
  • 02
    Phone banking From home or HQ. Scripts and lists provided. Ideal for evenings, accessible for those who can't door-knock.
  • 03
    Sign installation & repair Lawn-sign installs and repairs across the ward. Driver's licence and access to a vehicle preferred.
  • 04
    Literature drops Walking routes through targeted polls. Great for fitness; manageable for couples and families.
  • 05
    Data & digital Entering canvass results, social media posting, design work, video editing. Remote-friendly.
  • 06
    Election day GOTV Driving voters to polls, scrutineering, lit drops, phone banking. The all-hands-on-deck day.

Authorized by the Chief Financial Officer of the Shawn Allen Campaign.

Contributions to municipal campaigns in Ontario are governed by the Municipal Elections Act, 1996. Only individuals normally resident in Ontario may contribute. Corporations and trade unions are prohibited from contributing. The maximum contribution to a single candidate is $1,200; the maximum aggregate contribution to candidates for Toronto City Council is $5,000. Contributions of more than $100 are publicly disclosed in the financial statement filed with the City Clerk after the election. Contributions are not tax deductible.

Toronto residents who contribute may be eligible for a rebate from the City of Toronto under Toronto Municipal Code, Chapter 53. The official rebate is determined by the City after the election and may differ from estimates shown on this site.
